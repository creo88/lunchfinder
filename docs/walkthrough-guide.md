# Walkthrough with Mark and Timothy (step 3)

The goal of this meeting is one answer and one commitment, not a demo:

> "If this were your real cohort, could you drop this into your next funder
> report or board deck as-is?"

**Pass:** he would use it unchanged, or names at most 1–2 edits, **and** agrees
to a next step in writing (pilot, letter of intent, or a stated budget figure).
**Fail:** he asks for a different set of numbers, or says his funders need a
format we didn't produce. Then ask what the format is and rebuild around it.

## Before you book

- [ ] **Ask Mark for one real past funder report** (or the funder's reporting
      template) and the board deck page where program results go. Comparing the
      one-pager against it before the meeting is the cheapest way to avoid
      "my funders need a different format".
- [ ] **Decide what you are offering.** Add-on to the in-person cohort, or the
      first piece of a cheaper online program? You will ask for a budget figure,
      so know the range you'd accept before you walk in.
- [ ] **Put his organization's name in.** Open the dashboard → *Data and settings*
      → *Partner organization*. The PDF and CSVs pick it up.
- [ ] Print the PDF one-pager (two copies) and have the dashboard open.
- [ ] Book 30 minutes with **both** Mark and Timothy. Timothy is still verbal
      only; this meeting is where that changes.

## Agenda (30 minutes)

| Time | What | Notes |

| 8–13 | Dashboard, briefly | Switch to one cohort; show the copy-paste summary; download the CSV and open it in his spreadsheet. |
| 13–14 | **Ask the question** | Word for word, then stay quiet. |

| 24–30 | Ask for the next step | See "Asking for the commitment". |

## Follow-up questions (after he answers)

- "What would you change before it went to a funder?" Count the edits.
- "Which report would this go into first, and when is it due?"
- "Which of these five numbers does your board ask about most?"
- "Is anything here you'd be uncomfortable showing a funder?"
- "Who else has to say yes before you commit? The board? When do they meet?"
- To Timothy: "What budget would this come out of, and roughly what's in it?"

## Asking for the commitment

Make the ask specific and small enough to say yes to in the room:

> "Here's what we'd propose: one cohort of about {25} participants starting
> {month}, with this dashboard and the one-pager for your {funder/board} report.
> Could you put that in a short email or letter of intent this week, with the
> budget you'd plan for it?"

If he can't commit without the board, ask for the board date and offer to make
the one-pager the board pre-read.

## Same-day follow-up email

> Subject: AI program reporting: next step
>
> Mark, Timothy, thanks for your time today.
>
> What we heard: {his answer, the 1–2 edits}.
> What we agreed: {pilot / LOI / budget figure}, by {date}.
>
> Could you reply to confirm, or send the letter of intent? We'll make the
> edits and send an updated one-pager by {date}.

A reply to this email is the written next step the pass criteria ask for.

## If it fails

Don't argue the format. Ask for the actual template or a past report, and which
funder it's for. Rebuild the one-pager around it (`src/pdf.js`) and book a
second, shorter look within two weeks.
